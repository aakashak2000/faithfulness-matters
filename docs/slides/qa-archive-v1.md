# Likely questions and one-sentence answers (draft v1, 2026-10-20)
Each answer names its basis. Verify any number against its file before the talk.

| # | Likely question | One-sentence answer | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why is your first model below the published baseline? | It reads one utterance with frozen encoders and no dialogue context; bcLSTM reads the whole conversation. We then built stronger models (63.6) that pass it, so the weights are tested on a model that is at least on par. | MELD paper Table 11 (59.25); `results/baselines_summary.csv` (51.7); `results/f1_push_v2.csv` (63.6) |
| 2 | Doesn't the confidence rule do much better (+0.225)? | Part of that is structural: it stays +0.225 when every model is trained on shuffled labels, and on the gate's own model the two cannot be told apart (+0.006 [-0.017, +0.028]). | `results/shuffled_control.csv`, `results/paired_gate_vs_confidence.csv` |
| 3 | Does the gate beat "always text"? | At the 0.5 cutoff no (+0.019 [-0.003, +0.041]); with a cutoff fitted on dev it does on test (+0.049 [0.035, 0.062]). | `results/resampling_full_faithfulness.csv`, `results/dev_calibration_test.csv` |
| 4 | Did you tune the cutoff on test? | No: it is fitted per seed on dev and frozen; the idea came from looking at the test calibration curve, and the interval is conditional on the fitted cutoff. | `scripts/19_dev_calibration.py` |
| 5 | Does the answer depend on how you measure the effect? | Yes: +0.019 with the pre-registered effect (change in the predicted class's probability), +0.09 with the change in the whole distribution, +0.06 with label flips; we report the first as primary and the others as sensitivity. | `results/alt_effect_metrics.csv` |
| 6 | Why did you change the primary intervention? | Blank stand-ins are far from real data (cosine similarity 0.30 vs 0.68 for the mean), so we promoted swapping in real examples after seeing the mean and blank results and before seeing its own; mean and blank stay as checks. | `results/embedding_distances.json`; step 7 plan |
| 7 | Isn't removing a modality out of distribution? | Yes, which is why we swap in real examples and average; the blank result (-0.032) we treat as a stand-in artifact. | `results/resampling_full_faithfulness.csv` |
| 8 | Does "zero" mean no effect or too little data? | At the 0.5 cutoff the interval rules out gains above about +0.04. | same file, upper bound 0.041 |
| 9 | What about conflicting text and audio? | A pilot found no drop in gain under conflict (+0.008 [-0.018, +0.034]), but a second random pairing disagreed, so we need about 10 pairings. | `results/conflict_h2_diff.csv` |
| 10 | Is this seed luck? | No: a 10-seed rerun gives +0.020 with per-seed values +0.010 to +0.036. | `results/more_seeds_faithfulness.csv` |
| 11 | How is this different from Hessel & Lee? | Same swap-and-average idea; they ask whether a model needs both modalities together to be accurate, we ask whether a model's own weight matches how much each modality changes its prediction. | `docs/slides/slide-01-sources.md` |
| 12 | Isn't the weight by definition the contribution? | It is the share of the vote; importance is how much the answer would change without it, which also depends on how distinctive that model's vote is. | worked example in the session; `results/gate_fixed_summary.json` |
| 13 | Do your findings apply to the other papers' models? | No, only to our model on MELD; they show the reading can fail in a measurable way, so it needs testing. | scope in `docs/PROJECT_REQUIREMENTS.md` |
| 14 | Did you build the model or use an existing one? | We built it: frozen MiniLM and WavLM-large embeddings, a linear head each, and a learned gate and confidence rule on top. | `scripts/02_train.py`, `scripts/common.py` |
| 15 | Hasn't modality reliance already been measured with interventions? | Yes (Hessel & Lee 2020; Gat et al. 2021; Parcalabescu & Frank 2023 measure how much a model relies on each modality); our narrower question is whether the weight a fusion model reports agrees with that kind of measurement, per utterance. | `docs/slides/slide-01-sources.md`; prep notes |
| 16 | Is 0.37 a property of the weight? | No: it is an empirically fitted decision cutoff, chosen on dev data for this model; it has no intrinsic meaning. | `results/dev_calibration_thresholds.csv` |
| 17 | What is the takeaway so far? | The weight is informative but misread at its default cutoff: at 0.5 it matches the swap test 79% of the time against 77% for always guessing "text"; a cutoff fitted on development data reaches 82% on test. | `results/resampling_full_faithfulness.csv`, `results/dev_calibration_test.csv` |
| 18 | What is the input, the output, and where do your labels or ground truth come from? | Input: an utterance's text and audio; output: an emotion and a text weight. The emotion labels are MELD's; our faithfulness measure does not use them: its reference is the swap test, which uses only how the model's own output changes. | `docs/PROJECT_REQUIREMENTS.md` section 2 |
| 19 | How do you know the swap test is the right reference? | It is our operational definition, not causal ground truth; we check it three ways of removing a modality and three ways of measuring the change, and report how the size of the result moves (for example +0.019 vs +0.09). | `results/alt_effect_metrics.csv`, `results/resampling_full_faithfulness.csv` |
| 20 | Isn't this too simple, or an artifact? | Simple on purpose, so the weight has an exact meaning; the work is in the controls: ten trained models, a shuffled-label control, a held-out test and three removal methods. | `results/more_seeds_faithfulness.csv`, `results/shuffled_control.csv` |
| 21 | Where does the improvement come from? | From reading the weight at the right cutoff, not from new information: weights between about 0.37 and 0.5 are mostly cases where text still mattered more. | `results/gate_calibration_primary.csv` |
| 22 | What was technically hardest? | Removing a modality without feeding the model unrealistic inputs (hence real examples averaged), and never choosing a cutoff on the test set. | `scripts/08_resampling_full.py`, `scripts/19_dev_calibration.py` |
| 23 | Is 79% good? Do you have human validation? | 79% only means something against the 77% baseline; there is no human validation yet, which is a limitation. | `results/resampling_full_faithfulness.csv` |
| 24 | How do I read this chart? | The x-axis is the text weight, the y-axis is how often the swap test said text mattered more; if the weight meant what it says, the curve would cross 50% at 0.5, and it crosses near 0.37. | `figures/fig3b_gate_calibration_primary.png` |
| 25 | Isn't this just because your audio model is weak? | We checked with a stronger audio model (audio-only weighted-F1 46.3 instead of 36.8): the gate's weight then matches the swap test 93% of the time against 64% for the best trivial rule, and the best cutoff moves from 0.37 to about 0.63. So the verdict depends on model strength; we report both and treat the stronger one as a preliminary variant (one run; the gate's input also changed). | `results/better_audio_faithfulness.csv`, `results/strong_audio_checks.csv` |
| 26 | So does a stronger audio model make the weight more faithful? | Not in agreement terms: the weight matches the swap test about 79% of the time whether the audio model scores 36.8 or 46.7. What changes is the trivial guess: it falls from 77% to about 54% as audio contributes more, so the weight's edge grows from 2 to about 24 points. | `results/quality_ladder.csv`, `figures/fig5_quality_ladder.png` |
| 27 | Why is your best model different from your headline model? | The headline model was fixed in advance. The stronger audio heads were designed afterwards, with the choice made on development data, and are reported as variants; each rung has one run. | `results/audio_grid_extension.csv`, `results/better_audio_head.csv` |
| 28 | Did the change in the gate matter? | Yes: when we fed the gate 8,192 standardised features, its weights became extreme and agreement jumped to 93%; with the headline gate input the agreement stays near 79%. We report the controlled version. | `results/quality_ladder.csv` |
| 29 | Does a stronger model make the weight more faithful? | Not by itself. With natural models the weight's edge over the best trivial guess is about +0.18 to +0.26 when text and audio are comparably strong, +0.06 to 0 when text dominates, and negative when audio dominates; artificially flattening one model's scores does not recreate the edge, because the gate adapts. | `results/quality_ladder.csv`, `results/swap_strong_text.csv`, `results/balance_experiments.csv` |
| 30 | Are your models competitive with the published baseline? | Yes against bcLSTM 59.25 and DialogueRNN 60.25: our strongest scores 63.6 (RoBERTa-large text, WavLM audio, dialogue context, dev-tuned class-prior correction); recent systems reach 67 to 70, so we are not state of the art, and the comparison is not controlled because our encoders are newer. | `results/f1_push_v2.csv`; `docs/slides/comparison-table.md` |
| 31 | If the weight is not importance, what is? | Roughly the weight times how far that model's vote is from a typical vote; this one-pass signal matches the swap test 94 to 99% of the time across our models (plain weight 75 to 97%, and no better than a trivial guess in the lopsided ones). It is the closed form of the swap, so it explains the mismatch rather than independently confirming it. | `results/corrected_signal.csv` |
| 32 | Why not just match the state of the art? | Our question is whether weights match removal effects, not accuracy; we needed a sound model, at or above the published baseline, to test them on. | `docs/slides/comparison-table.md` |
| 33 | Which systems score higher on MELD? | MERC-PLTAF 67.73 and MiSTER-E 69.5 (text and audio only, MiSTER-E with fine-tuned 8B and 7B language models), and TelME 67.37 and others with video added. | `docs/slides/comparison-table.md` (read in each paper) |
| 34 | Do those papers test their fusion weights? | None of the papers we read does; the 8-paper gating review found weights described as importance but never tested by removal (a targeted search, not a full survey). | `docs/slides/slide-01-sources.md`, `comparison-table.md` |
| 35 | Do other datasets show better text + audio models? | Yes, IEMOCAP scores are 70 to 75 (MERC-PLTAF 74.80, MiSTER-E 70.9, TelME 70.48) and MiSTER-E reaches 87.9 on CMU-MOSI, but these are different tasks and labels, so scores are not comparable to MELD. | `docs/slides/comparison-table.md` |
| 36 | Is 63.6 a fair number? | It uses dialogue context (including later utterances) and a class-prior correction fitted on dev, not test; without context and correction the same models score about 56 to 61. | `results/f1_push_v2.csv` |
